# Processing for AI

AI systems need processing hardware to prepare data, run models, train neural networks, and generate predictions. Different processors are designed for different kinds of work: CPUs provide flexible control, GPUs perform many similar calculations in parallel, and specialized accelerators optimize particular AI workloads.

In this section, we explore:

- [How do CPUs support AI workloads?](01-How-do-CPUs-Support-AI-Workloads/README.md) — how CPUs coordinate applications, prepare data, and support AI systems.
- [How do GPUs accelerate AI?](02-How-do-GPUs-Accelerate-AI/README.md) — why GPUs are effective for matrix operations, model training, and large-scale inference.
- [What are AI accelerators?](03-What-are-AI-Accelerators/README.md) — specialized hardware such as TPUs, NPUs, ASICs, FPGAs, IPUs, wafer-scale engines, and dataflow accelerators.

Understanding these processing options helps you choose hardware based on the model, available memory, latency requirements, power budget, software support, and cost.
